Risk parity: under a third of the drawdown of equal weight, with a higher Sharpe ratio

Six ETFs covering Chinese stocks, treasury bonds, gold and three commodity futures funds, weighted so each one contributes the same share of portfolio risk. Rebalanced every 30 trading days, after trading costs, from Oct 2013 to Feb 2026.

What I did: built a risk parity portfolio across six asset classes, compared it with equal weight, 60/40 and mean-variance optimization, all after trading costs.

Result: Sharpe ratio of 1.00 and a 4.8% max drawdown, against 0.78 and 16.1% for equal weight. Mean-variance looked best in hindsight but reached only 0.46 when run live.

      Compared with mean-variance optimization

      Mean-variance optimization picks the weights with the highest expected return for each level of risk, which traces out the efficient frontier. Using Feb 2020 to Feb 2026 data for all six ETFs, risk parity sits just under the frontier, close to the long-only maximum Sharpe portfolio.

      The catch: the frontier needs returns you only know afterwards

      In a live portfolio the optimizer has to estimate expected returns from the past. I ran both optimizers on the same schedule as risk parity, every 30 trading days with 0.08% costs per side, estimating returns from the past year and covariance from the past 60 days.

      Method

      At each rebalance I estimate the covariance matrix from the past 60 days of log returns. Asset i's risk contribution is its weight times its marginal risk, wi(Σw)i/σ. SciPy's SLSQP solver finds long-only weights that sum to one and make all contributions equal, and the script checks that they come out equal.

      Between rebalances the weights drift with prices. Costs of 0.08% per side are charged on turnover, the sum of the weight changes, so all four portfolios pay for what they trade.

      Limitations

      • Bonds averaged about three quarters of the portfolio, so the result leans on a long bull market in Chinese government bonds. Rising rates would hurt it.
      • A 5.5% return at 3.6% volatility is low in absolute terms. Funds that run risk parity use leverage to reach a target return, and this test leaves out leverage and its financing cost.
      • A 60-day window reacts quickly but makes the weights noisy. Longer windows or shrinkage of the covariance matrix are worth testing.
